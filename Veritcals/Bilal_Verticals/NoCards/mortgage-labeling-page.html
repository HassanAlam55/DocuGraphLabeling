Document types

Built for every mortgage format

Domain-trained models for the loan origination, title, and settlement documents your team actually works with.

📝

Loan Applications (Form 1003)

Borrower and co-borrower names, Social Security number locations, employment history, monthly income, assets, liabilities, property type, loan purpose (purchase, refinance, construction), loan amount, interest rate type (fixed, ARM), amortization term, down payment amount, and declaration responses parsed across Uniform Residential Loan Application formats from all major lenders.

📄

Closing Disclosures & Loan Estimates

Loan terms (amount, interest rate, monthly P&I), projected payments, closing cost breakdowns (origination charges, services borrower did/did not shop for, taxes, government fees), cash to close calculations, APR, total interest percentage (TIP), escrow account details, and TRID-required disclosure fields extracted across the CFPB-standardized five-page Closing Disclosure format.

🔍

Title Search & Commitment Reports

Property legal description, current owner (vestee), vesting type (joint tenancy, tenants in common, community property), recorded liens and encumbrances, easements, restrictive covenants, tax lien status, judgment searches, title exceptions (Schedule B items), effective date, commitment number, and title company underwriter details identified across title search and commitment formats from major underwriters.

🏠

Property Appraisals (Form 1004)

Subject property address, legal description, neighborhood characteristics, site dimensions and zoning, improvement description (bedrooms, bathrooms, gross living area, year built, condition, quality), comparable sales data (sale prices, dates, adjustments), reconciled appraised value, effective date, and appraiser license number extracted across URAR (Uniform Residential Appraisal Report) and other GSE-accepted formats.

📜

Deed Transfers & Mortgage Notes

Grantor and grantee names, deed type (warranty, quitclaim, grant, special warranty), property legal description, parcel number (APN), consideration amount, recording information (book, page, instrument number), notary acknowledgment blocks, mortgage note terms (principal, rate, maturity date, prepayment provisions), and rider attachments (ARM rider, condominium rider, PUD rider) structured for closing automation and document verification pipelines.

💰

HUD-1 & Settlement Statements

Settlement date, property location, borrower and seller names, gross amount due from borrower, adjustments for items paid by seller, total settlement charges, real estate broker commissions, loan origination fees, government recording charges, transfer taxes, title insurance premiums, escrow deposits (property taxes, hazard insurance, mortgage insurance), and daily interest charges extracted for pre-TRID transactions and refinance closings that still use the HUD-1 format.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Everything you need to know before you start.

What is mortgage and title document labeling?
Mortgage and title document labeling annotates loan applications, closing disclosures, title reports, appraisals, and other real estate transaction documents with structured labels that ML models use as training data. Each region is tagged with its semantic role (borrower names, property addresses, loan amounts, interest rates, lien positions, legal descriptions, closing dates, escrow amounts) and exported in a format your pipeline can consume directly.
Which mortgage and title document types are supported?
Uniform Residential Loan Applications (Form 1003), Closing Disclosures (CD), Loan Estimates (LE), HUD-1 Settlement Statements, title search reports, title commitment letters, title insurance policies, property appraisal reports (Form 1004/URAR), deed transfers (warranty, quitclaim, grant, special warranty), mortgage notes, trust deeds, lien release documents, payoff statements, and escrow instructions.
How accurate is automated mortgage document labeling?
Labels are typically 93%+ accurate on standard mortgage document layouts. Closing disclosures and loan applications follow CFPB-regulated formatting standards that are highly consistent across lenders, which allows the model to achieve strong baseline accuracy. Confidence scores attach to every label so low-confidence regions can be flagged for manual review before underwriting automation or compliance verification runs.
What export formats are supported?
Structured JSON with bounding boxes, segment text, label classifications, and confidence scores. Markdown is also supported. Both are directly compatible with PyTorch DataLoaders, TensorFlow Datasets, and Hugging Face Transformers.
Is the tool free?
Yes. Label your first 5 pages without an account. Larger batches and API access are available on paid plans.
Is sensitive mortgage and borrower data secure?
Yes. Documents are processed over encrypted connections, never used for model training without explicit consent, and can be deleted immediately after labeling. Enterprise plans include SOC 2 compliance, private deployment options, and data handling protocols suitable for mortgage lenders, title companies, settlement agents, fintech platforms, and government housing agencies with strict GLBA and TRID compliance requirements for borrower financial records.
Can this help with mortgage underwriting and title processing automation?
Yes. The labeling output structures borrower names, property addresses, loan amounts, interest rates, APR values, monthly payments, closing costs, lien positions, legal descriptions, title exceptions, escrow amounts, and closing dates into machine-readable formats that feed directly into automated underwriting systems, title examination platforms, closing process automation tools, and mortgage compliance dashboards that accelerate loan origination, title clearance, and settlement processing.
