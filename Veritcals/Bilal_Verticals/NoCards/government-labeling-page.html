FAQ

Questions, answered

Everything you need to know before you start.

What is government and public records labeling?
Government and public records labeling annotates civic, legal, and administrative documents with structured labels that ML models use as training data. Each region of the document is tagged with its semantic role (case numbers, filing dates, jurisdictions, agency names, statute citations, property identifiers, permit details) and exported in a format your pipeline can consume directly.
Which government document types are supported?
FOIA requests and responses, court records and case filings, vital records (birth, death, and marriage certificates), property deeds and land records, building and business permit applications, government contracts and procurement documents, legislative bills, regulatory filings, and public meeting minutes.
How accurate is automated government document labeling?
Labels are typically 92%+ accurate on standard government document layouts. Confidence scores attach to every label so low-confidence fields can be flagged for manual review before records digitization, compliance checks, or model training runs.
What export formats are supported?
Structured JSON with bounding boxes, segment text, label classifications, and confidence scores. Markdown is also supported. Both are directly compatible with PyTorch DataLoaders, TensorFlow Datasets, and Hugging Face Transformers.
Is the tool free?
Yes. Label your first 5 pages without an account. Larger batches and API access are available on paid plans.
Is sensitive government data secure?
Yes. Documents are processed over encrypted connections, never used for model training without explicit consent, and can be deleted immediately after labeling. Enterprise plans include SOC 2 compliance, private deployment options, and data handling controls suitable for government environments subject to FedRAMP, FISMA, and state-level data governance regulations.
Can this help with public records digitization and FOIA automation?
Yes. The labeling output structures document sections, entity fields, and metadata into machine-readable formats that feed directly into records management systems, FOIA response automation pipelines, and civic data portals that make public information searchable and accessible.
