FAQ

Questions, answered

Everything you need to know before you start.

What is court and judicial document labeling?
Court and judicial document labeling annotates case filings, court orders, legal pleadings, judgments, and motions with structured labels that ML models use as training data. Each region of the document is tagged with its semantic role (case numbers, party names, judge names, court jurisdictions, filing dates, legal citations, ruling outcomes, relief sought) and exported in a format your pipeline can consume directly.
Which court and judicial document types are supported?
Complaints and petitions, answers and responsive pleadings, motions and briefs, court orders and rulings, judgments and decrees, docket sheets and case registers, subpoenas, discovery requests and responses, settlement agreements, appellate briefs and opinions, and administrative hearing decisions.
How accurate is automated court document labeling?
Labels are typically 93%+ accurate on standard court document layouts. Court filings follow procedural formatting rules set by federal and state court systems (FRCP, local court rules), which allows the model to achieve strong baseline accuracy. Confidence scores attach to every label so low-confidence regions can be flagged for manual review before legal analytics or model training runs.
What export formats are supported?
Structured JSON with bounding boxes, segment text, label classifications, and confidence scores. Markdown is also supported. Both are directly compatible with PyTorch DataLoaders, TensorFlow Datasets, and Hugging Face Transformers.
Is the tool free?
Yes. Label your first 5 pages without an account. Larger batches and API access are available on paid plans.
Is sensitive court and case data secure?
Yes. Documents are processed over encrypted connections, never used for model training without explicit consent, and can be deleted immediately after labeling. Enterprise plans include SOC 2 compliance, private deployment options, and data handling protocols suitable for law firms, courts, legal tech companies, and government agencies with strict confidentiality requirements for sealed filings, juvenile records, and attorney-client privileged materials.
Can this help with legal analytics and case management automation?
Yes. The labeling output structures case numbers, party names, judge assignments, filing dates, legal citations, cause of action codes, ruling outcomes, damages awarded, and procedural history into machine-readable formats that feed directly into legal analytics platforms, case management systems, litigation tracking dashboards, and judicial workload analysis tools that monitor docket activity, predict case timelines, and surface outcome patterns automatically.
