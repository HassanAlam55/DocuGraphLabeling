10 Best Data Labeling Tools for PDF Documents in 2026

 

Most data labeling tools were built for images first. Then text. Then video. PDFs were an afterthought — bolted on later as a "supported format" that usually means converting your document to a flat image and hoping the layout survives.

That matters because PDF documents are not images. A contract has headers, clauses, signature blocks, and exhibit references that carry structural meaning. A financial statement has tables with row-column relationships that collapse the moment you flatten the page. A medical record has sections that need to be labeled differently depending on whether they contain patient demographics, clinical notes, or lab results.

If you are building document AI models — for layout analysis, key-value extraction, document classification, or information retrieval — the labeling tool you choose determines whether your training data preserves that structure or destroys it.

We evaluated over 20 labeling platforms against the specific requirements of PDF document labeling: native PDF handling (not image conversion), layout-aware segmentation, bounding box accuracy, domain-specific label taxonomies, ML-ready export formats, and whether the tool can actually get you from a raw PDF to a usable training dataset without writing custom preprocessing code.

Here are the 10 that handle PDF documents best in 2026.

How We Evaluated These Tools

Every tool on this list was assessed against six criteria specific to PDF document labeling — not the generic feature checklists you find on most comparison pages.

1. Native PDF handling. Does the tool process PDFs as structured documents, or does it convert them to flat images first? Image conversion destroys text searchability, table structure, and character-level positioning. For data labeling tasks that require layout-aware annotations, native PDF processing is not optional.

2. Auto-labeling quality. Can the tool automatically detect and label document regions (headers, paragraphs, tables, lists, footers) without manual intervention? And if so, how accurate is it out of the box — before any model customization or training data feedback?

3. Domain-specific label support. Legal contracts, financial statements, medical records, and insurance claims each have their own structural conventions. Does the tool offer pre-configured label taxonomies for specific document types, or does it force you to build your taxonomy from scratch?

4. Export format compatibility. The labeled data needs to reach your training pipeline. We evaluated whether exports work directly with PyTorch DataLoaders, TensorFlow Datasets, and Hugging Face Transformers — or whether you need custom preprocessing scripts to bridge the gap.

5. Bounding box and coordinate precision. Document AI models like LayoutLMv3 and Donut require normalized bounding box coordinates in [x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max] format. We checked whether each tool exports coordinates in a format compatible with standard document AI architectures, or whether coordinate transformation is required.

6. Pricing and accessibility. Some teams need to label 50 PDFs to prototype a model. Others need to label 50,000 for production training. We noted which tools let you start immediately without a sales call, and which require enterprise contracts before you can upload your first document.

 

1. DocuGraph (AI Asset Management)

Best for: Teams that need to go from a raw PDF to a labeled, ML-ready dataset in minutes — not weeks.

Website: aiasset-management.com/datalabeling/ 

DocuGraph is the only tool on this list that was built specifically for PDF document labeling from day one. Not image annotation with PDF support added later. Not text labeling with an OCR layer on top. The entire platform — from the segmentation engine to the export pipeline — was designed around the structural complexity of multi-page PDF documents.

What sets it apart:

The auto-labeling engine is the headline feature. Upload any PDF, a legal contract, a financial statement, a medical record, an immigration petition, and DocuGraph's AI segments the document into logical regions (headers, paragraphs, tables, lists, signature blocks, exhibit labels) and applies classification labels automatically. Labeling accuracy exceeds 90% out of the box on standard document types, which means the majority of your annotation work is review and correction, not manual labeling from scratch.

That 90% figure matters more than it sounds. In traditional annotation workflows, the labeling step itself — drawing bounding boxes, assigning labels, reviewing adjacency relationships — consumes 60 to 80 percent of total ML project time. Cutting that to a review-and-correct workflow changes the economics of document AI projects entirely.

DocuGraph also offers pre-configured domain models for legal, financial, medical, insurance, immigration, real estate, tax, and academic documents. Each model comes with a label taxonomy built for that document type, so you are not starting from a blank canvas every time you work with a new industry vertical. If the built-in taxonomies do not fit your use case, you can define custom labels and apply them across your document set.

Privacy architecture:

DocuGraph's Zero-Retention Processing Mode processes documents entirely within the browser. No document data is uploaded to any server, written to any disk outside the user's machine, or retained after the session ends. For teams in regulated industries — legal, financial, healthcare, government — this is the feature that gets the tool past security review. You can verify the zero-transmission claim independently using your browser's network inspector.

Export and integration:

Exports include JSON with full bounding box coordinates (normalized [x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max]), Markdown, Hugging Face Datasets format, and Delta Lake tables for Databricks. The JSON schema is directly compatible with PyTorch DataLoaders and Hugging Face Transformers for training LayoutLMv3, Donut, Faster R-CNN, and custom document AI architectures — no preprocessing scripts required.

Pricing: Free. No account required to start. Label your first five pages immediately. No credit card. No sales call.

Limitations: Currently supports PDF documents up to 50 pages. Teams that need to label datasets of tens of thousands of documents across distributed annotator teams may need to combine DocuGraph's auto-labeling with a workflow management layer. The API is in early access and not yet generally available.

 

2. Label Studio

Best for: Technical teams that want full control over their annotation infrastructure and are comfortable with self-hosting.

Label Studio is the most widely adopted open-source data annotation platform, and for good reason — it supports more data types and annotation configurations than any other free tool. For PDF documents, Label Studio converts pages to images with an OCR layer, then lets you annotate using bounding boxes, named entity recognition spans, text classification, and relation extraction.

The flexibility is genuine. You can define custom labeling interfaces using Label Studio's template language, plug in external ML models through its ML Backend for pre-labeling, and integrate with Hugging Face or OpenAI for LLM-assisted annotation. The recent addition of interactive LLM Prompt mode allows multi-step annotations (NER + classification + QA) in a single workflow.

The trade-off is that Label Studio treats PDFs as paginated images with text overlays, not as structured documents. The original PDF structure — reading order, table relationships, nested headers — is flattened during conversion. For tasks that require extracting tables from PDFs with preserved row-column relationships, this can be a significant limitation.

Pricing: Open-source (free, self-hosted). Label Studio Enterprise adds team management, RBAC, analytics, and SSO at enterprise pricing.

 

3. Prodigy

Best for: NLP practitioners who already work in the spaCy ecosystem and want a fast, scriptable annotation workflow.

Prodigy is a commercial annotation tool from Explosion (the company behind spaCy) that emphasizes speed and active learning. Its Prodigy-PDF plugin extends the core platform with workflows for loading, rendering, and annotating PDF documents — including OCR extraction and page-level grouping for multi-page documents.

What makes Prodigy different from most tools on this list is its philosophy: it is a developer tool, not a GUI platform. Annotation workflows are defined as Python "recipes" that you run from the command line. This makes it extremely flexible for teams that want to script custom annotation pipelines, but it also means there is no drag-and-drop interface for non-technical annotators.

Active learning is built into the core workflow. Prodigy's model-in-the-loop approach selects the most informative examples for human review, so annotators spend their time on the cases the model is least confident about rather than labeling documents the model can already classify correctly.

Pricing: One-time license fee of $490 per annotator. No subscription, no recurring fees.

 

4. UBIAI

Best for: Named entity recognition on native PDF documents where preserving the original layout is critical.

UBIAI is one of the few commercial platforms that handles native PDF annotation — meaning it processes the PDF directly rather than converting it to an image first. This preserves character-level positioning, table structure, and layout information that are important for training NLP models on semi-structured documents like invoices, contracts, and forms.

The platform supports NER, relation extraction, and document classification within the same interface, and it lets you create nested and overlapping entity annotations — a capability that most tools either lack or handle poorly. UBIAI also offers auto-annotation features to speed up the labeling process.

For teams working on PDF data extraction tasks where layout fidelity directly affects model performance, UBIAI's native PDF handling is a meaningful differentiator.

Pricing: Free tier available with limitations. Paid plans start at $19/month.

 

5. Labelbox

Best for: Enterprise teams managing large-scale labeling operations across multiple data types with workforce management needs.

Labelbox is a full-featured enterprise data labeling platform that supports images, video, text, geospatial data, and documents — including PDFs. Its strength is not PDF-specific functionality but rather the operational layer it wraps around the annotation process: project management, quality assurance workflows, consensus scoring, and model-assisted labeling pipelines.

For PDF document labeling specifically, Labelbox provides document annotation interfaces and model-assisted pre-labeling. The platform integrates with custom ML models for automated labeling suggestions, and its Catalog feature lets you search, filter, and curate large document datasets before annotation begins.

The trade-off is complexity and cost. Labelbox is built for teams running annotation programs at scale with multiple annotators and quality reviewers. If you need to label a few hundred PDFs for a prototype model, the platform's enterprise infrastructure is more than you need.

Pricing: Limited free tier. Enterprise plans require a sales conversation.

 

6. Encord

Best for: Teams working with complex document datasets alongside medical imaging, video, or LiDAR data.

Encord positions itself as an AI data development platform rather than a pure annotation tool. Its document annotation capabilities are wrapped inside a broader data management ecosystem that includes Encord Index (dataset curation and search), Encord Annotate (labeling workflows), and Encord Active (model evaluation).

For PDF documents, Encord supports annotation with complex ontologies — nested classifications, multi-level attributes, and conditional labeling — that handle the structural depth of legal, financial, and healthcare documents. The platform is SOC 2 Type II certified, which matters for teams in regulated industries.

Where Encord shows particular strength is when document annotation is part of a larger multi-modal project. If your pipeline involves labeling PDFs alongside DICOM medical scans or satellite imagery, Encord handles all three within one platform. If you only need PDF labeling, the platform's breadth works against simplicity.

Pricing: Limited free tier. Paid plans based on usage and team size.

 

7. V7

Best for: Teams that need visual annotation with strong auto-labeling on visually complex documents.

V7 (formerly V7 Darwin) is a computer vision-focused platform that has expanded into document annotation. Its standout feature is SAM 3 integration — Segment Anything Model version 3 — which enables text-prompt-based automatic detection and segmentation of document regions. Type a class name, and SAM 3 detects and segments all instances in the document.

For PDF documents with complex visual layouts — architectural drawings, product catalogs, marketing materials with mixed text and graphics — V7's visual segmentation capabilities are strong. The platform also supports multi-stage review workflows with conditional logic and consensus scoring.

V7 is SOC 2 Type II certified and HIPAA compliant, and it supports a wide range of export formats including JSON, COCO, YOLO, and Pascal VOC. For document AI teams specifically, the COCO format export works directly with Detectron2 training pipelines.

Pricing: Limited free tier. Paid plans based on annotation volume.

The right tool depends on three things: what you are building, how much data you need to label, and who is doing the labeling.

If you need to label PDFs for document AI model training and want to start in the next five minutes, DocuGraph is the fastest path from raw PDF to usable training data. Upload a document, review auto-generated labels, export to your framework of choice. No account, no setup, no cost.

If you want full infrastructure control and have a technical team that can manage self-hosting, Label Studio gives you the most flexibility at zero cost. You will need to invest time in configuration, OCR setup, and ML backend integration, but the customization ceiling is the highest on this list.

If you need native PDF handling with NER and relation extraction, UBIAI and DocuGraph both process PDFs natively rather than converting to images. UBIAI is stronger for pure NER tasks; DocuGraph is stronger for full document segmentation with auto-labeling.

If your project combines PDF documents with other data types — medical imaging, video, satellite imagery — Encord and V7 handle multi-modal datasets within a single platform.

What Makes PDF Labeling Different From Other Data Types

Most guides on data labeling tools treat PDF documents as a subset of "text annotation" or "image annotation." That framing misses the core challenge. PDFs are layout-dependent, multi-modal documents where the spatial relationship between elements carries meaning that pure text or pure image annotation workflows destroy.

A table in a PDF is not a list of text strings — it is a grid of cells with headers, row labels, and values that need to be understood as a unit. A heading is not just bold text — it is a structural marker that defines the hierarchy of everything below it. A signature block is not a paragraph — it is a discrete region with a specific function in a legal workflow.

When a labeling tool converts a PDF to a flat image, it preserves the visual appearance but loses the text layer, the character-level coordinates, and the reading order metadata that the PDF format encodes. When it converts a PDF to raw text, it preserves the words but loses the spatial layout, the table structure, and the visual separation between sections.

Effective PDF labeling requires preserving both — the visual layout and the structural semantics. That is why we weighted native PDF handling so heavily in our evaluation criteria, and why tools that treat PDFs as just another file format rank lower than tools that were built for PDF documents specifically.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best free data labeling tool for PDF documents?

DocuGraph is the most capable free option for PDF document labeling. It offers AI-powered auto-labeling with 90%+ accuracy, native PDF processing, domain-specific models for legal, financial, medical, and insurance documents, and export to JSON, Markdown, Hugging Face Datasets, and Delta Lake — all with no account required. Label Studio is the best free open-source option if you need full infrastructure control and are comfortable with self-hosting and configuration.

What is the difference between data labeling and data annotation?

Data labeling and data annotation are often used interchangeably. Both refer to the process of adding structured tags, classifications, or metadata to raw data so it can be used for machine learning model training. In the context of PDF documents, labeling typically means segmenting a document into regions (headers, paragraphs, tables) and assigning each region a classification label. Annotation can also include drawing bounding boxes, marking named entities, or defining relationships between document elements.

Can I label PDF documents without uploading them to the cloud?

Yes. DocuGraph's Zero-Retention Processing Mode processes documents entirely within the user's browser. No document data is uploaded to any server, stored on any external disk, or retained after the session ends. This matters for teams in regulated industries (legal, financial, healthcare) that cannot upload sensitive documents to third-party cloud infrastructure. You can verify the zero-transmission claim using your browser's built-in network inspector.

What export format do I need for training document AI models?

Most document AI architectures — LayoutLMv3, Donut, Detectron2 — expect labeled data in JSON format with normalized bounding box coordinates in [x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max] format. If you are using Hugging Face Transformers, the Hugging Face Datasets format allows direct loading via datasets.load_dataset(). If your training pipeline runs on Databricks, Delta Lake format lands labeled data directly in governed lakehouse tables. DocuGraph supports all four export formats natively.

How many labeled documents do I need to train a document AI model?

It depends on the task and the model architecture. For fine-tuning a pre-trained model like LayoutLMv3 on a specific document type, teams commonly start with 200 to 500 labeled documents and iterate from there. For training a model from scratch, thousands to tens of thousands of labeled documents are typically required. The more accurately your labeled data reflects the structural variations in your actual document corpus, the fewer examples you need. Starting with a well-designed labeling taxonomy reduces the total volume needed.

Do I need separate tools for labeling and for training?

Typically, yes. Labeling tools produce the training data; training frameworks (PyTorch, TensorFlow, Hugging Face Transformers) consume it. The quality of the integration between the two determines how much manual work falls between them. Tools that export in formats directly compatible with your training framework — like DocuGraph's native JSON, Hugging Face Datasets, and Delta Lake exports — eliminate the custom preprocessing scripts that otherwise sit between annotation and training.
